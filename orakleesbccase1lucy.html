Introduction

Early-Stage, Hormone Receptor-Positive, HER2 2+ (FISH Negative) Breast Cancer

History of Present Illness

Welcome. Your patient today is Lucy K.

Lucy is a 59-year-old postmenopausal woman with no prior history of breast cancer or high-risk genetic mutations who underwent a routine screening mammogram last week, which identified an abnormality in the left breast.

She has no history of prior breast biopsies and no known family history of breast or ovarian cancer. She has been generally healthy, with a medical history significant for mild hypertension, well-controlled on medication, and osteopenia diagnosed 5 years ago.

She has 2 adult children, had her first pregnancy at age 29, and breastfed both children. She has been on postmenopausal hormone therapy (estrogen only) for 5 years, which she recently stopped at her physician’s recommendation.

She has never smoked, drinks wine occasionally, and has no history of significant occupational or environmental exposures associated with breast cancer. She maintains an active lifestyle, walks daily, and follows a balanced diet.

She denies breast pain, nipple discharge, skin changes, or palpable lumps.

Physical Examination Notes

There is no palpable mass, nipple discharge, pain, or skin changes.

Case Review

This case underscores the importance of integrating clinical judgment, genomic profiling, and a patient-centered approach to avoid unnecessary treatment. Genomic testing, such as MammaPrint and Oncotype DX, confirmed this patient’s ultralow-risk status and low recurrence risk, reinforcing that chemotherapy would not provide significant benefit, consistent with outcomes from large prospective trials and current international guidelines . This case also highlights the evolving interpretation of HER2 testing, particularly for tumors classified as HER2 IHC 2+ with FISH negative, which are now considered HER2-negative/HER2-low per ASCO–CAP criteria. In this context, reliance on genomic risk rather than HER2 IHC score alone is critical to avoid overtreatment with chemotherapy or HER2-targeted agents when no clinical benefit is expected . Accordingly, careful interpretation of HER2 results helps prevent overtreatment in cases where HER2-targeted therapy is not warranted.

Due to her ultralow-risk classification and low recurrence risk, the patient did not receive chemotherapy; she completed APBI and is now on endocrine therapy alone. This case supports the role of genomic profiling in HER2 IHC 2+ (FISH negative; ie, HER2-negative/HER2-low) breast cancer management, challenging the traditional paradigm that all HER2 IHC 2+ cases require chemotherapy and HER2-targeted therapy.

Key Takeaways

Genomic profiling (e.g., MammaPrint, Oncotype DX) can refine treatment decisions .

Not all HER2 IHC 2+ (FISH negative) tumors behave aggressively; some may have an indolent course .

Chemotherapy can be avoided in carefully selected ultralow-risk, low-recurrence-risk patients .

Personalized treatment strategies can optimize outcomes while minimizing unnecessary toxicities .

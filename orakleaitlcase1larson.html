Introduction

History of Present Illness

Welcome. Your patient today is Ms. H. Larson

Ms. H. Larson is a 71-year-old woman with a history of polymyalgia rheumatica, maintained on low-dose corticosteroids for approximately two years, who presents with a several-month history of progressively enlarging lymph nodes in multiple regions. She first noticed fullness in one axilla while dressing; over the ensuing weeks, she noticed additional, similarly enlarged nodes in the opposite axilla and along the neck. The swellings have been painless, and she denies antecedent upper-respiratory illness, recent travel, new medications, or sick contacts. Her appetite has been stable. She reports feeling “slower” than usual but continues daily activities independently. There is no history of autoimmune flares during this period and no change in her steroid dose.

On review of systems, she denies easy bruising or bleeding, recurrent infections, or new rashes. She has not noticed drenching night sweats; sleep has been unremarkable apart from intermittent awakenings to use the bathroom, which she attributes to age. No pruritus, chest pain, dyspnea, cough, abdominal pain, or change in bowel habits.

The physical examination is remarkable for generalized lymphadenopathy, most appreciable in the cervical and axillary chains; nodes were firm, mobile, and non-tender. There were no focal neurologic deficits, and the remainder of the exam was noncontributory. Given the persistence and distribution of adenopathy in an older adult with a history of chronic low-dose corticosteroid use, further evaluation was pursued.

Case Review

Recognizing angioimmunoblastic T-cell lymphoma (AITL):

AITL is a peripheral T-cell lymphoma derived from follicular helper T cells, often presenting in older adults with generalized lymphadenopathy, immune dysregulation, and constitutional symptoms. Unlike B-cell lymphomas, it may feature a polymorphous infiltrate with reactive B-cell expansion, sometimes with EBV-positive elements, making diagnosis dependent on integrated histologic, immunophenotypic, and molecular assessment .

Diagnostic integration:

A definitive diagnosis requires correlation between clinical findings and tissue pathology. Key immunophenotypic features include CD4⁺ T cells expressing multiple TFH markers such as PD-1, ICOS, CD10, CXCL13, and BCL6, along with characteristic vascular proliferation. Molecular testing for TET2, DNMT3A, RHOA, and IDH2 mutations strengthens diagnostic confidence and provides insight into clonal evolution (Xie Y, et al, 2021; d’Amore F, et al. 2025).

Therapeutic considerations:

Treatment selection is guided by CD30 expression and comorbidity profile. For patients with CD30⁺ disease (≥10% neoplastic T cells), brentuximab vedotin plus CHP (BV-CHP) is the preferred first-line regimen. For CD30⁻ or low-expressing cases, CHOP remains standard. Supportive measures—growth factor support, antimicrobial prophylaxis, and close biochemical and imaging monitoring—optimize tolerability and disease control (NCCN, 2025; d’Amore F, et al. 2025).

Prognostic and supportive implications:

The disease course is typically aggressive, and relapse rates remain high, emphasizing the importance of multidisciplinary follow-up, including infectious monitoring, cardiac surveillance, and repeat imaging. Emerging evidence supports exploring novel combinations, including epigenetic modifiers or checkpoint-based strategies, particularly for relapsed/refractory disease (Ong & Zain, 2024).

Epidemiology & Clinical Spectrum

Angioimmunoblastic T-cell lymphoma (AITL) is a mature T-cell neoplasm within the nodal TFH group, typically presenting in the 6th-7th decade with painless, generalized lymphadenopathy, constitutional symptoms, and immune dysregulation (e.g., hypergammaglobulinemia, autoimmune cytopenias). EBV-positive B-cell proliferations and Hodgkin/Reed-Sternberg-like cells are frequent and can confound diagnosis if not interpreted in context (Xie & Jaffe, 2021). Contemporary reviews and guidelines highlight its heterogeneity and the frequent advanced stage at presentation in older adults (Ong & Zain, 2024; d’Amore F, et al. 2025).

Classic AITL spans Patterns I-III, progressing from subtle perifollicular expansion with regressed follicles (pattern I) to diffuse effacement with arborizing high-endothelial venules and extrafollicular FDC expansion (pattern III) (Xie & Jaffe, 2021).

Prognostic Overview

Despite heterogeneity, overall outcomes remain modest, with median overall survival ~2-3 years and 5-year survival ~30-40% in population-based and real-world series (Li S, et al. 2024; d’Amore F, et al. 2025; Ong & Zain, 2024). Prognosis correlates most strongly with age > 60 years, performance status, and response to initial therapy rather than histologic subtype. These data reinforce the need for early molecular risk stratification and clinical-trial referral where feasible.

Histopathology & TFH Immunophenotype

AITL-classic histology includes polymorphous infiltrates, proliferation of arborizing high-endothelial venules, and expansion of follicular dendritic-cell (FDC) meshworks. The neoplastic cells are typically small-to-medium CD4⁺ T cells of TFH derivation with expression of ≥2-3 TFH markers (PD-1, ICOS, CD10, CXCL13, BCL6). Loss of pan-T antigens (e.g. CD3, CD7) is common and supports aberrancy. Because EBV-positive B-cell expansions are part of the microenvironment, integrating the TFH panel with morphology avoids misclassification as a B-cell process . Guidelines reinforce using a broad TFH IHC panel to raise diagnostic confidence and to distinguish AITL from PTCL-NOS and FTCL (d’Amore F, et al. 2025; NCCN, 2025).

Molecular Pathogenesis & Diagnostic Integration

Recurrent mutations in TET2 and DNMT3A (epigenetic regulators) often precede T-cell-restricted lesions such as RHOA G17V and IDH2 R172, framing AITL as a multistep process that starts in hematopoietic progenitors and culminates in TFH transformation and B-cell dysregulation. Additional hits in TCR-signaling (e.g. PLCG1, VAV1, CD28) further shape the phenotype. The presence of overlapping mutations in tumor-associated B cells underscores a shared clonal origin for elements within the AITL microenvironment. A multiparametric approach—clinicopathologic correlation + TFH immunophenotype + targeted molecular testing—improves sensitivity, particularly in early/type I patterns that can mimic reactive disease (Xie & Jaffe, 2021). Reviews and guidelines recommend targeted NGS when available to support classification and trial eligibility (Ong &Zain, 2024; d’Amore F, et al. 2025; NCCN, 2025).

Differential Diagnosis

Key differentials include PTCL-NOS, FTCL, classic Hodgkin lymphoma with abundant TFH cells, and reactive paracortical hyperplasia. A structured algorithm—morphology, TFH marker panel, EBV assessment, and clonality/genomics—minimizes pitfalls, especially when EBV-positive HRS-like cells dominate the biopsy (Xie & Jaffe, 2021; NCCN, 2025).

Staging & Risk Assessment

Guidelines support standard lymphoma staging with PET/CT and bone marrow evaluation as indicated, integrating LDH, performance status, and extranodal sites into risk appraisal. In older adults, geriatric-oncology assessment (frailty, comorbidities, polypharmacy) guides intensity of systemic therapy and supportive care (NCCN, 2025; d’Amore F, et al. 2025).

Bone-marrow involvement occurs in up to 70 % of cases, typically showing loose paratrabecular/interstitial aggregates. Flow cytometry in BM or peripheral blood can detect small sCD3-/dim CD4⁺ TFH clones and provides a useful baseline for monitoring. (Xie & Jaffe 2021).

Prognostic Scoring Systems

Conventional IPI retains prognostic value, but modified indices such as the PIT and AITL-specific models incorporating LDH, performance status, and bone-marrow involvement offer better risk separation (d’Amore F, et al. 2025). Elevated LDH and advanced stage independently predict inferior survival.

Frontline Therapy Framework

Frontline selection pivots on CD30 expression in the neoplastic T-cell component. If clearly positive (commonly ≥10%), BV-CHP is preferred; when CD30 is absent or unconfirmed, CHOP remains guideline-concordant. In responders, autologous SCT in CR1 may be discussed case-by-case depending on age, fitness, and response depth; allogeneic SCT is generally reserved for selected R/R scenarios given toxicity in older patients (NCCN, 2025; d’Amore F, et al. 2025; Ong J, Zain J, et al. 2024; Horwitz SM, et al. 2018).

Consolidation and Transplant Strategies

In fit, chemosensitive patients, autologous SCT in first complete remission (CR1) is guideline-supported as a consolidation option; however, its overall-survival benefit remains uncertain. Notably, the Lymphoma Study Association (LYSA) propensity-matched cohort in nodal PTCL reported ~60% 5-year OS in both ASCT-intended and non-ASCT groups, showing no OS advantage for upfront ASCT. Candidate selection should therefore be individualized by depth of response, age/frailty, comorbidities, and patient preference, with delayed transplantation generally reserved for relapsed/refractory disease (Fossard G, et al. 2018; NCCN, 2025; d’Amore F, et al, 2025).

Relapsed/Refractory Landscape

For R/R disease in older adults, belinostat and pralatrexate are established single-agent options; BV monotherapy can be considered in CD30-positive settings depending on local policy. Epigenetic combinations (e.g. azacitidine-based) show biologic rationale in TFH-type disease due to the epigenetic mutation profile, but remain off-label and are best pursued in trials (Ong &Zain, 2024; d’Amore F, et al. 2025).

Supportive Care & Monitoring

Older AITL patients benefit from proactive febrile-neutropenia prevention with G-CSF, infection prophylaxis (e.g. HSV/VZV ± PJP per risk), and cardiac surveillance with anthracycline exposure. EBV DNA monitoring may assist when prominent EBV-positive B-cell/HRS-like proliferation is present. Interim and end-of-treatment PET/CT (Lugano) inform continuation, consolidation, or trial enrollment decisions (NCCN, 2025; d’Amore F, et al. 2025; Moskowitz AJ, 2019).

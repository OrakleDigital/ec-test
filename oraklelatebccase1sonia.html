Introduction

History of Present Illness

Welcome. Your patient today is Sonia A.

A 65-year-old woman with a history of stage IIIC (T2 N3a M0) invasive ductal carcinoma of the left breast presented with a new left supraclavicular mass. Her initial diagnosis involved a 5.0 x 3.4 cm subareolar mass with extensive axillary lymph node involvement, confirmed by bilateral breast magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and ultrasound-guided core biopsy. The tumor was characterized as estrogen receptor-positive (ER+) at 92%, progesterone receptor-positive (PR+) at 63%, and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)/neu negative, with a high proliferation index (Ki-67 of 92%). A staging workup, including positron emission tomography-computed tomography (PET-CT), showed no evidence of distant metastases. She received neoadjuvant chemotherapy with FEC (fluorouracil, epirubicin, cyclophosphamide) followed by paclitaxel, leading to tumor size reduction.

Subsequent treatment included a left modified radical mastectomy and a right simple mastectomy with immediate implant reconstruction. Pathology revealed a residual 1.9 cm tumor, with 16 out of 17 axillary lymph nodes positive for metastatic disease. Postoperatively, she underwent two cycles of adjuvant chemotherapy with gemcitabine and carboplatin, followed by radiation therapy. She was maintained on anastrozole as part of her adjuvant hormonal therapy until she detected nodularity in the left supraclavicular region.

Case Review

Late recurrence in breast cancer, particularly in hormone receptor–positive (HR+) disease, remains a major challenge in long-term patient management. Unlike other subtypes, HR+ breast cancer is characterized by a persistent risk of relapse that extends well beyond 5–10 years after initial diagnosis, reflecting the ability of disseminated tumor cells to enter a state of prolonged dormancy and later reactivate . These late recurrences are frequently metastatic and clinically significant, underscoring the need for sustained vigilance even after prolonged disease-free intervals. Improved understanding of endocrine sensitivity, resistance mechanisms, and tumor biology has highlighted the importance of individualized, long-term treatment planning rather than time-limited risk assessment.

From a clinical perspective, managing late recurrence requires a balance between extended surveillance, patient education, and thoughtful therapeutic sequencing when relapse occurs. International consensus guidelines emphasize that HR+/HER2− metastatic disease—including late isolated or oligometastatic recurrences—should be approached as a systemic condition, with endocrine therapy–based strategies remaining the foundation of care, complemented by targeted agents when appropriate . As treatment options continue to evolve, incorporating biomarker-driven therapies and novel agents, ongoing research into cancer dormancy and late relapse biology will be essential to refine prevention strategies, optimize sequencing, and ultimately improve long-term outcomes for patients living with HR+ breast cancer.

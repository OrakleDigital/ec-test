Introduction

A 62-Year-Old Female with Chronic Pain, Fatigue, and Worsening Mobility

History of Present Illness

Welcome. Your patient today is Antonia R

Antonia R, a 62-year-old post-menopausal female, presents with persistent chronic joint pain, bone pain, difficulty walking long distances without taking a rest, as well as fatigue, which has progressively worsened over the years. She denies any history of fractures but reports a strong family history of similar symptoms among relatives, including her father and 2 siblings. Over the last several months, she has experienced worsening depressive symptoms, which she attributes to her declining quality of life (QOL) and chronic health struggles.

Medical History

  • Antonia struggled with activities requiring stamina and strength during childhood, such as gym class and team sports, due to pain in her bones and joints.
  • She experienced premature loss of primary dentition, followed by malalignment of permanent teeth, necessitating extensive dental interventions as an adult.
  • Chronic muscle weakness that began in adolescence was initially dismissed as “growing pains.”
  • She was diagnosed with fibromyalgia at age 54 after years of diffuse pain, difficulty walking, and fatigue, although treatment has been largely ineffective.
  • She has a longstanding history of fatigue, which she attributes to her chronic pain and poor sleep quality.
  • Antonia reports daily use of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs for several years, providing some relief for joint pain but no effect on fatigue.
  • She experienced menopause at the age of 50, has no menopausal symptoms, and is not on any hormone supplementation.
  • At the age of 50, during a routine female wellness check, Antonia R.’s laboratory results revealed a low alkaline phosphatase (ALP) level of 23 U/L; however, no further evaluation or follow-up was conducted at that time.

Antonia’s lifelong trajectory of symptoms, coupled with her family history, suggests an undiagnosed underlying condition contributing to her chronic pain, fatigue, and functional limitations. These details warrant a thorough diagnostic evaluation to identify the cause and make the appropriate diagnosis.

Family and Social History

Antonia reports a notable family history of health issues. Her father experienced chronic joint pain, which was attributed to arthritis, and also struggled with anxiety. Antonia notes that several relatives on her father’s side have dealt with progressive muscle weakness and fatigue, although no formal diagnosis was ever established.

Socially, Antonia is retired and lives alone. Previously active in her local community, she now spends less time with friends due to her chronic pain and fatigue, leading to increasing social isolation. She does not smoke, drinks alcohol socially (1–2 glasses of wine per week), and denies any history of illicit drug use.

Case Review

This case highlights the complexity of diagnosing a rare metabolic bone disorder such as HPP. The patient’s clinical history of early primary tooth loss, chronic musculoskeletal weakness, coupled with diagnostic findings including persistently lowered ALP (see chart below), elevated vitamin B6, support a diagnosis of HPP. Although the patient’s X-ray findings were unremarkable, the clinical presentation and biochemical abnormalities are sufficient to establish a working diagnosis of HPP in this case.

The genetic test eventually returned a positive, c. 571G>A p. (E191K) is a pathogenic variant confirming an ALPL gene mutation, further supporting the diagnosis.

Clinical pearls

Persistently Low ALP activity is a red flag, as they may indicate an underlying metabolic disorder such as HPP . ALP plays a critical role in bone mineralization , and persistently low levels may result in impaired bone metabolism, leading to symptoms such as skeletal abnormalities, fractures, and muscle weakness. Low ALP activity warrants further investigation to identify the cause and confirm the diagnosis.

HPP is a progressive condition with evolving manifestations, which result in significant morbidity across all age groups. .

Adult HPP is marked by fractures, pseudofractures, chronic pain, tooth loss, muscle weakness, and joint calcifications from calcium pyrophosphate (PPi) deposits . Early tooth loss is a key sign across all forms of HPP .

The progressive nature of HPP underscores the importance of early diagnosis to better characterize its effects across all age groups.

The burden of Disease and Impact on QOL

Functionally, mobility is often significantly impaired, with many patients reporting decreased walking ability after diagnosis. HPP may also impact mental health, with common challenges like sleep disturbances, mood issues, and anxiety, all contributing to reduced QOL .

From an economic perspective, HPP results in high individual and societal healthcare costs along with a significant loss of productivity and income, particularly for adults with severe disability .

Activities of daily living are greatly restricted, with patients reporting difficulties in physical functioning and role-based activities. Overall, HPP imposes a substantial physical, emotional, and social burden, severely affecting QOL.

These burdens are reflected in QOL metrics, where patients consistently report lower scores across physical, emotional, and social dimensions of QOL assessments.
